# Lean Personal Planner

Lean Personal Planner is a project and workload planning tool designed around two core principles:

- **Workload leveling (Heijunka):** distribute work evenly over time to avoid overloaded days and weeks.
- **Continuous improvement:** track what actually happened, understand variations, and use that information to make better plans in the future.

The goal is not to create a perfect long-term schedule, but to build a realistic plan that continuously adapts as work progresses.

## Getting Started

### 1. Clear the Example Workspace

Click **Sync & Backup** in the top-right corner, then select **Clear Workspace** to remove the example data and start with a clean workspace.

### 2. Set Up Your Master Schedule

Start on the **Master Schedule** tab.

Create a list of all your projects and add their respective deadlines. This will give you an overview of your commitments and the time constraints you need to work around.

### 3. Break Down Your Projects (WBS)

Go to the **WBS** tab.

Break down each project into the main steps you believe are necessary to complete it. At this stage, rough estimates and broad steps are perfectly fine.

- Add constraints whenever a step cannot be performed until another step has been completed.
- Include your colleagues' tasks as well as your own.
- For tasks performed by colleagues, set **Responsibility** to `Others`.

The purpose is to understand the overall sequence of work required to complete each project, not to define every individual action just yet.

### 4. Build Your Weekly Work Plan

Go to the **Weekly Work Plan (WWP)** tab.

First, add your fixed activities, such as lunch breaks, meetings, and other recurring commitments.

Then, for each project, start from its final deadline and work backwards. Place your steps in reverse chronological order, starting with the last steps required before the deadline.

Don't worry if your schedule is still relatively empty. Having only a few 4-hour tasks per week is perfectly fine at this stage.

Planning several months ahead is inherently uncertain. We will progressively improve the accuracy of the schedule as we get closer to each deadline.

### 5. Refine the Current Week (Heijunka)

Go back to the current week in the WWP and look at what you **WILL** have to do.

You may find that fitting everything into your schedule is difficult. For now, simply place your upcoming tasks on the calendar, then switch to the **Heijunka** page.

Here, you can break large tasks into smaller, more manageable chunks. Since these tasks are approaching soon, you should have enough information to make a more detailed breakdown.

If you cannot break a task down further, this is a good opportunity to investigate what is still unclear.

Once your tasks are sufficiently detailed, look at the histogram to identify overloaded days.

### 6. Level Your Workload

Workload leveling is the core principle of this application.

Return to the WWP and rearrange your tasks for the week. Your objective is to distribute work as evenly as reasonably possible across the different days, as visualized on the Heijunka page.

Don't force it too much. The goal is not mathematical perfection, but a sustainable and realistic workload.

Once your mandatory tasks are distributed, you can add tasks you **SHOULD** be working on to fill the remaining capacity.

Repeat the leveling process until you have a reasonably balanced week.

### 7. Prepare the Following Week

Once your current week is reasonably organized, I recommend preparing the following week as a **Look Ahead Schedule**.

This gives you visibility into upcoming commitments and helps you anticipate potential overload before it becomes a problem.

## Daily Use and Continuous Improvement

### 8. Track Your Actual Progress

As the week progresses, mark completed tasks as done.

If something did not happen as expected, select **Done – Not as Expected** and explain the reason for the variance.

Try to understand what went wrong rather than simply recording that a task was delayed or took longer than expected.

If you don't know what caused the variation, take the time to investigate it. Understanding why your plan failed is essential to making a better one next time.

### 9. Review Your Statistics

The **Statistics** tab provides an overview of your work history.

Use it to understand:
- What went according to plan.
- What did not go as expected.
- Why variations occurred.
- Where your planning estimates could be improved.

The objective is to learn from your past performance and progressively improve the reliability of your planning.

### 10. Keep Your Schedule Updated

You should use this application every day.

Whenever something unexpected happens, adjust your future schedule accordingly.

For example, if an unexpected task needs to be completed this week but there is no room for it, consider postponing a **SHOULD** task to the following week.

Your schedule should reflect reality, not an outdated plan.

## A Note on Hour-by-Hour Scheduling

Although the WWP uses an hour-by-hour schedule, the goal is not necessarily to enforce precise working hours.

If your organizational skills allow you to follow such a schedule, feel free to try. However, don't fly too close to the sun: humans are not machines, and work is subject to systematic variations and delays.

The hour-by-hour schedule is primarily a tool to help you determine whether your tasks can realistically fit around your fixed activities.

**What matters most is that tasks you committed to completing on a given day are completed on that day — not before, and not after.**

The exact hour of completion does not matter as much as the time required to complete the task.

If a task takes longer than expected (which is very common), it was not performed *as expected*. Record the variance and its cause so that you can avoid making the same planning mistake in the future.

Remember: the purpose of planning is not to predict the future perfectly, but to continuously improve your ability to anticipate and manage it.
